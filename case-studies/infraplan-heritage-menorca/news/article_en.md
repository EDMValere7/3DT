# Where cars can't go: Scanfly EVO captures Menorca's historic towns

**Narrow alleys, pedestrian zones and three islands in Maó harbor. To complete the digital twin of three historic towns in the Balearic Islands, Infraplan and 3D Target put Scanfly EVO to the test. The survey was completed in under three days, with accuracy better than 10 cm.**

## The project

Infraplan is a leading Spanish geospatial solutions provider. Founded more than twenty years ago, it specializes in advanced mapping and surveying for urban planning, infrastructure management, environmental monitoring and heritage conservation.

The assignment covered three towns in Menorca: Maó (Mahón), Ciutadella and Es Castell. The first two are the island's most populous towns and were founded by the Carthaginians. Maó, the capital, sits on one of the longest natural harbors in the world, 5 kilometers long and up to 900 meters wide. Es Castell, a smaller town on the eastern side of the harbor, was founded later by the British and takes its name from a large fort nearby.

## The challenge

Most of the survey had been carried out with a car-mounted mobile mapping system. But a car can't enter pedestrian zones or the narrow streets of a historic center, and it certainly can't reach the three islands in front of Maó harbor that were part of the job.

Completing the model called for a system that could go anywhere without compromising accuracy.

## The solution: one system, multiple platforms

Infraplan and 3D Target ran a Proof of Concept with **Scanfly EVO**, 3DT's multi-platform mobile mapping system, combined with SLAM-enabled processing.

With the **Land Mounting Kit**, Scanfly EVO switches from car roof to backpack quickly, with no tools required. Starting from a KML file of the missing areas, 3DT's support team split the survey into several sessions:

- **by bike, wearing the backpack**, to cover the accessible urban areas quickly;
- **on foot**, for pedestrian-only zones;
- **by boat, then on foot**, to reach and scan the three islands, including the historic hospital on Isla del Rey.

## Time and accuracy

Data capture was completed in **under three days**, including travel between sites and boat transfers to the islands.

Each session's trajectory was processed with RINEX files from a local CORS network for the most accurate positioning. Where GNSS visibility was poor, trajectories were further refined with the SLAM algorithm in **SmartProcessing Lidar**, 3DT's proprietary software for generating point clouds and panoramic images.

The result is a 3D model of the areas of interest with **accuracy better than 10 cm**, measured against ground control points.

## Why it matters

The Proof of Concept highlighted two clear advantages of Scanfly EVO:

- **compared with bulkier mobile mapping systems** that can only be mounted on a vehicle roof, its multi-platform design lets a single instrument reach every part of the project;
- **compared with handheld LiDAR scanners that rely on SLAM alone**, backpack mode delivers a more efficient workflow. Capture is faster, with no speed limitation, and the expected accuracy is reached with the same workflow in every survey condition, without ground control points.

Detailed, high-accuracy 3D data is essential for urban documentation and heritage reconstruction. It helps preserve, restore and plan, and changes how cities and historic sites are studied and developed.
